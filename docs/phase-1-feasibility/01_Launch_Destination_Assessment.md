# Launch Destination Assessment

## Status and method

- Assessment date: 2026-07-29
- Purpose: choose a bounded operational launch area, not the eventual nationwide scope
- Scoring: relative 1–5 judgment for MVP feasibility; it is not a tourism forecast
- Assumption: the solo founder can perform or arrange local verification in the selected area

The assessment considers demand, domain diversity, authorized-source and image feasibility, verification effort, seasonality, competition, and the ability to test the full MVP. Official portals show that Thailand’s open-data platform includes Tourism Authority datasets for attractions and events, but each dataset’s license and currency still require inspection before use ([Data.go.th TAT catalog](https://www.data.go.th/th/organization/tat), [DGA open-data guidance](https://data.go.th/pages/about-open-data)).

## Candidate boundaries

- **Chiang Mai:** Mueang Chiang Mai core, initially bounded to Old City, Night Bazaar/riverside, and Nimman–Santitham corridors; exclude distant district excursions until separately activated.
- **Bangkok:** a central zone spanning selected adjacent neighborhoods only; exact district boundary would require founder approval.
- **Phuket:** one connected tourist zone rather than the full island.
- **Alternative — Ayutthaya:** historic-island area plus a small surrounding urban service radius.

## Chiang Mai core

### Strengths

- Strong mix of temples, museums, neighborhoods, food specialties, markets, walking streets, crafts, and local experiences.
- Compact areas can test discovery, itinerary planning, event recurrence, and map handoff without citywide coverage.
- Active public-sector and tourism ecosystem. Chiang Mai hosted Thailand Travel Mart Plus 2025 with international participation, indicating meaningful foreign-tourism relevance ([Chiang Mai Province](https://www.chiangmai.go.th/managing/public/newsshow/13407)).
- Official provincial publishing includes festivals and market activity, useful as leads for direct authorization and verification ([Chiang Mai event example](https://www.chiangmai.go.th/managing/public/newsshow/14289)).
- More differentiated “local companion” proposition than central Bangkok.

### Risks

- Severe seasonal air-quality risk can affect trip safety, demand, and recommendation quality.
- Event pages are not automatically licensed for copying or images.
- The wider province is geographically large; “nearby areas” must not silently expand the launch boundary.
- Food and hidden-gem claims require direct business or expert verification.

### Feasibility conclusion

Best balance of product richness and bounded operational scope, provided the founder can verify locally and accepts an air-quality/weather operating policy.

## Central Bangkok

### Strengths

- Highest domain density, extensive hospitals and assistance services, broad food and attraction diversity, strong international demand.
- Good environment for multilingual and emergency-directory testing.

### Risks

- Very high record churn, duplicate-place risk, address complexity, and event volume.
- A “central zone” can feel arbitrary and may confuse tourists traveling outside it.
- Intense competition from established travel and map products.
- Verification and freshness workload is too high for a solo-founder first launch unless the catalog is extremely small.

### Feasibility conclusion

Strong future destination but not the preferred first operational area.

## Bounded Phuket tourist zone

### Strengths

- Strong foreign-tourist demand and clear tourism use cases.
- Emergency, weather, beach safety, nightlife, transport, and tourist-assistance scenarios provide useful testing.
- Business imagery and English information may be easier to solicit from tourism operators.

### Risks

- Island geography encourages users to expect coverage beyond a small zone.
- Monsoon, marine hazards, transport variability, and rapidly changing commercial information increase safety and maintenance burden.
- Higher risk of the product drifting toward bookings and commercial listings.

### Feasibility conclusion

Viable second-stage destination after emergency and weather operations are proven.

## Ayutthaya historic core

### Strengths

- Small, legible boundary with major cultural attractions and realistic same-day itineraries.
- Easier manual verification and lower content churn.
- Close enough to Bangkok for field verification and pilot recruitment.
- Good candidate for testing authorized heritage imagery and external map handoff.

### Risks

- Lower diversity of recurring events, nightlife, and multi-day itineraries.
- Fewer scenarios for broad hidden-gem and neighborhood exploration.
- Emergency directory still needs an adequate surrounding urban radius.

### Feasibility conclusion

Operationally easiest fallback, but less complete as a test of every product domain.

## Recommendation

**Recommended launch destination:** bounded central Chiang Mai, limited to a founder-approved polygon covering the Old City, Night Bazaar/riverside, and Nimman–Santitham areas.

**Fallback:** Ayutthaya historic island plus a defined urban emergency-service radius.

## Founder approvals and blockers

- Approve the exact launch polygon; “Chiang Mai and nearby areas” is too broad.
- Confirm practical access to local verification.
- Complete a source and image inventory before locking the destination.
- Validate emergency-service coverage inside and immediately outside the traveler boundary.
- Decide whether launch should avoid or explicitly handle the annual smoke season.
- If authorized sources or emergency coverage fail the activation standard, move to Ayutthaya rather than lowering the standard.
