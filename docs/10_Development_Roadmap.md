# Development Roadmap

## Delivery approach

Use evidence-driven phases with explicit gates. The architecture supports nationwide expansion, but destinations are activated only when their data, rights, safety, cost, and operational quality are ready. This roadmap defines sequence rather than calendar commitments.

## Phase 0 — Blueprint correction and approval

Current phase deliverables:

- Consistent product, architecture, data, AI, UX, admin, source, security, roadmap, and monetization documents
- Clear MVP and future-state separation
- Free tourist access
- External Google Maps and Apple Maps handoff
- Internal navigation and hotel booking exclusions
- First-class emergency and provenance requirements
- Solo-founder operational constraints

Exit gate: all 11 documents are approved. No application implementation begins in this phase.

## Phase 1 — Pre-coding feasibility

Resolve and document:

- Initial launch destination or bounded region
- Initial supported languages
- Authorized source availability for every core domain
- Real-place image rights and attribution
- Emergency-data coverage, authority, quality, and verification cadence
- Map-handoff terms and behavior
- AI, translation, weather, analytics, and media-provider terms
- Privacy and third-party processor risks
- Monthly AI, infrastructure, media, and content-maintenance cost ceiling
- Destination activation standard and solo-founder review capacity

Exit gate: required data can be lawfully and sustainably published; core emergency content meets its stricter standard; expected costs fit the approved ceiling; material privacy risks have an accepted treatment. Qualified review is obtained where needed.

## Phase 2 — Detailed product and technical design

Deliver:

- Prioritized MVP requirements and acceptance criteria
- Tested low-fidelity traveler and founder/editor flows
- Conceptual model refinement and physical data design
- Formal application contracts without implementation
- Architecture decision records for the smallest managed stack
- Source, license, image, emergency, correction, expiry, and takedown procedures
- AI intended-use policy and language-specific evaluation set
- Threat model, retention policy, test strategy, and provider budgets

Exit gate: the first thin vertical journey is testable, estimable, affordable, and approved.

## Phase 3 — Curated destination preparation

Prepare a small verified dataset for the approved destination:

- Places, restaurants, food specialties, attractions, and local experiences
- Events, occurrences, markets, walking streets, and activities
- Emergency and tourist-assistance records
- Authorized documentary images and approved decorative assets
- Provenance, licenses, verification, freshness, expiry, and publication state

Use manual or lightly assisted workflows. Do not build broad ingestion automation.

Exit gate: destination activation checks pass and the founder can sustain the review queue.

## Phase 4 — Thin vertical MVP

Build one end-to-end journey before broad development:

1. Start a guest trip.
2. Request a grounded itinerary.
3. Receive verified recommendations.
4. Inspect source and freshness.
5. Save a place.
6. Find an assistance record.
7. Hand a destination to Google Maps or Apple Maps.
8. Report incorrect information privately.
9. Correct and republish it through protected administration.

Exit gate: deterministic fallback, authorization, provenance, cost controls, and essential tests work across the complete path.

## Phase 5 — Limited private pilot

- Test with foreign travelers and Thai local reviewers.
- Measure grounding, citation validity, itinerary usefulness, language quality, emergency accuracy, accessibility, reliability, and cost per successful task.
- Exercise provider outage, AI budget exhaustion, stale records, license expiry, takedown, backup restore, and incident response.
- Enforce the monthly cost ceiling at the pilot gate.

Exit gate: agreed safety, source, usability, cost, and operational thresholds hold for invited users.

## Phase 6 — Public MVP

Launch only the approved destination and supported languages. Complete production, privacy, security, support, rollback, monitoring, source, license, image, emergency-data, and cost-readiness reviews.

Exit gate: accountable reviewers accept residual risks and rollback readiness.

## Phase 7 — Nationwide expansion

Expand one controlled dimension at a time:

1. Broader coverage within the launch region
2. An additional supported language
3. A second destination that passes the activation standard
4. Improved event or food coverage
5. Selective ingestion automation
6. Additional destinations and provinces

Nationwide architecture does not require nationwide data population or operations at launch.

## Deferred capabilities

Native mobile applications, real-time alerts, complex offline synchronization, public reviews, collaboration, partner portals, sponsored ranking, internal hotel booking, booking integrations, and advanced commercial systems are post-MVP. External hotel or affiliate referrals may be considered later under separate approval.

## Cross-cutting test strategy

Future implementation requires proportionate unit, contract, integration, end-to-end, accessibility, localization, security, data-quality, provenance, license, emergency, provider-failure, performance, cost-budget, and AI evaluation. Each pilot gate includes actual provider-usage and budget testing.

## Approval checklist before coding

- Launch destination and languages resolved
- Authorized sources and image rights confirmed
- Emergency coverage and freshness rules approved
- Free-user limits and monthly cost ceiling approved
- Privacy, retention, and provider reviews complete
- MVP and future scope accepted
- One responsive client and modular monolith confirmed
- Thin vertical journey and acceptance criteria approved
