# Content Review Capacity Plan

## Status

- Status: **Proposed**
- Evidence obtained: no timed production review study
- Evidence needed: two-to-four-week sample workflow with actual Chiang Mai records
- Reviewer: Codex recommendation
- Approval: Founder unresolved

Exact workload is unknown. The figures below are measurable pilot hypotheses, not staffing facts.

## Content workflow

1. **Intake:** identify candidate and source; reject prohibited acquisition paths.
2. **Rights review:** record authorization, license, attribution, permitted use, and exit.
3. **Record creation:** normalize Thai/English identity and stable facts.
4. **Assertion verification:** attach evidence to hours, dates, fees, contacts, addresses, and classifications.
5. **Translation review:** review names, aliases, summaries, and consequential fixed text.
6. **Image review:** verify subject, owner, rights, transformations, attribution, and context.
7. **Domain review:** apply emergency or event-specific rules.
8. **Publication:** named founder/editor approval.
9. **Maintenance:** freshness queue, corrections, expiry, cancellation, and takedown.

## Proposed weekly capacity

Assume 6–10 founder hours/week for content operations during pilot:

| Work | Proposed weekly allowance | Initial throughput hypothesis |
|---|---:|---:|
| Emergency rechecks | 2 hours | 10–20 critical assertions |
| Event/market review | 1.5 hours | 8–15 occurrences |
| Ordinary freshness checks | 1.5 hours | 15–25 records |
| New records | 2 hours | 3–6 complete records |
| Image/rights and translation | 1–2 hours | 5–10 assets or summaries |
| Corrections/incidents | 1 hour reserve | Variable |

These ranges must be measured with start/finish time, rework, evidence gaps, and interruption cost.

## Maximum sustainable catalog hypothesis

- Proposed initial public catalog: 100–150 core place/event/activity records plus the verified emergency set.
- Proposed hard planning maximum before evidence: 200 active records and 30 active/near-term event occurrences.
- Expansion stops before the maximum if freshness, rights, emergency, or correction service levels are missed.

Record count is not the only load driver. Volatile events and emergency assertions cost more than stable attractions.

## Queue priority

1. Critical emergency report or stale field
2. Active takedown or license expiry
3. Event cancellation/rescheduling within 14 days
4. Other stale volatile facts
5. Incorrect-information reports
6. New content

Lower-priority publication pauses whenever critical work breaches its target.

## Proposed escalation rules

- Credible critical error: suppress immediately and start incident procedure.
- Emergency queue older than one business day: pause all new content.
- Takedown deadline risk: suppress asset/record and pause related publication.
- More than 20 overdue ordinary reviews: freeze catalog growth.
- More than 5 unresolved near-term event checks: stop showing affected occurrences.
- More than 10 hours/week for four consecutive weeks: reduce catalog/boundary or obtain help.
- Any critical fabrication or unsupported publication: stop AI/content release and investigate.

All timing thresholds require founder approval and must not promise continuous on-call coverage that does not exist.

## Incorrect-information queue

Capture affected field, severity, reporter evidence, source conflict, received date, triage date, action, reviewer, and closure evidence. Reporter statements are signals, not self-verifying facts.

## Deferred automation

After the manual process is measured:

- Reminders and queue ordering
- License/freshness expiry alerts
- Source-change detection
- Duplicate suggestions
- Draft translation and extraction
- Batch import previews

Automation cannot approve rights, publish emergency facts, or resolve conflicting evidence.

## Revision method

For four pilot weeks, record minutes per task, records completed, rework, overdue items, incidents, and source-contact wait time. Recalculate sustainable catalog size from the slowest safety-critical week, not the best week.

## Stop-expansion conditions

Do not expand the boundary or destination when emergency coverage is incomplete, rights evidence is missing, ordinary freshness falls below the activation threshold, cost projection exceeds 70% of ceiling, correction backlog breaches policy, or founder workload exceeds the approved limit.
